# 12 — Intent override, properly

**Status:** merged (one variant), with three measured rejections
**Commit:** (this one)
**Owner:** Dialog + Ranking
**Tier:** 2 (MRR) — outcome is score-neutral; the value is robustness and a closed question

## What & why

Feature 11 cleared the hard colour/material slots on an override and measured exactly flat. That was
a partial implementation: the requirement the customer *withdrew* was still sitting in `evidence`
(which builds the query) and in `phrases` (which is half the reranker's score), so the agent went on
ranking against a preference the customer had explicitly abandoned. This feature finishes the job —
and the finishing turns out to mean *deliberately not* retracting, for a reason that only shows up
when you read how the simulator builds an override.

**The structural finding, and the reason everything else here follows from it.**
`behavior_for` (`evaluator/local_evaluator.py:74-86`):

```python
old_value = soft[-1] if soft else "I prefer a different style."
new_value = hard[0] if hard else "Please prioritize the target requirements."
```

Both values are drawn from the **target product's own intent card**. The "abandoned" preference is a
true attribute of the very product we are trying to find, and the override never changes which
product satisfies the customer — the target is fixed for the whole session. **The override is
cosmetic.** Acting on the retraction can therefore only destroy true evidence, and because the
private set is generated by this same function, that holds there too.

## Where the headroom actually is

Measured by replaying all 30 `intent_override` sessions and recording the target's rank each turn
against what was disclosed:

- **29/30 hit, 25 at rank 1.**
- **Mean hit turn 3.69, against a floor of 3.60** — the `override_applied` guard
  (`evaluator/local_evaluator.py:252`) discards every hit before the override turn, so 3.60 is the
  best achievable mean. There is 0.09 of a turn of slack in the whole scenario.
- Only **2 of 30** sessions ever withheld a top-10 target at or after the override turn, and in both
  the deferral **paid**: `public_0002` went rank 5 (withheld, turn 3) to rank 1 (turn 4), and
  `public_0052` rank 5 to rank 2. Disclosing more at the override turn would have *lost* MRR.
- The 4 non-rank-1 hits sit at ranks **2, 2, 7, 4**; the one miss, `public_0144`, is on CLAUDE.md's
  information-theoretically-unreachable list.

`public_0103` is the tell: it sits at **rank 4 on every single turn**, before and after the override.
Nothing about override handling moves it. Those 4 sessions are the rank-2-to-8 cohort of
`docs/features/09-optimization-headroom.md` — a *ranking* problem (gap 1, coverage has no length
normalization), not an override problem.

## Approach — what shipped

`observe()` now clears **all** of `SLOTS` on an `OVERRIDE_RE` match, not just `HARD_FILTER_SLOTS`,
then lets the override message refill them. `price_max` is a numeric filter on routes 1 and 2
(`starter/retrieval.py:384,391`), so a stale ceiling excludes candidates exactly as effectively as a
stale colour, and there was no reason for it to survive an override.

Clearing a filter only ever *widens* the candidate set, so unlike the rejected variants below it
cannot **exclude** the target from contention. Be precise about what that does and does not buy:
widening admits competitors, so it can still change the target's **rank**. It removes a way to lose
the target outright; it is not a guarantee of no effect. On this set it has none — it fires in 0 of
30 sessions, so its ranking effect here is **unmeasured rather than proven harmless**.

## Measured impact

All four variants were run against `results_after_fieldfactors.json` (TechnicalScore 0.912205).

| Variant | What it does | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **A** (feature 11) | clear colour + material | flat, **byte-identical**; fires in **21/30** sessions |
| **B — shipped** | also clear `price_max` | flat, **byte-identical**; fires in **0/30** here |
| **C — rejected** | delete the retracted claim from `evidence` **and** `phrases` | **−0.003875** TechnicalScore |
| **D — rejected** | drop the retracted claim from phrase routes only, keep it for reranking | flat, but discards identifying routes |

**Variant C, the literal reading of "honour the retraction":**

| Metric | Before | After | Delta |

| Scenario | n | Metric | Before | After | Delta |

| intent_override | 30 | MTTC | 3.93333 | 4.03333 | +0.1 |
| boundary / browsing / buying | 170 | all | — | — | 0 |

The damage is confined to the scenario it "fixes". The withdrawn text is a true attribute of the
target, so deleting it deletes evidence that was helping rank the target once the pool was narrow.

**Variant D** was the attempt to keep that benefit while stopping the withdrawn claim from *widening*
the pool: exclude it from phrase routes (whole-catalog searches) but keep it for the reranker (which
only reorders an already-narrow pool). It measured flat and byte-identical — but instrumenting it
showed it is not inert:

- it changes `phrase_routes` output in **16 of 30** sessions;
- **19** retracted claims are rare enough (df ≤ `PHRASE_DF_MAX` = 2000) to have opened a route, and
  many have **df = 1** — `public_0004` "Long torso camisole for extra cov" (df 1), `public_0038`
  "Lace Slip On Sneaker" (df 2), `public_0125` "HOOK & LOOP CLOSURE…" (df 4).

A df=1 phrase route puts the target at rank 1 of a 1-item list. Variant D throws those away for zero
measured gain, and on the private set a session whose only identifying evidence is such a phrase
would turn from a hit into a miss. **Flat on the public set is not the same as safe.** Rejected.

## Limitations & follow-ups

- **The shipped change fires in 0/30 public sessions.** No override session carries a price ceiling,
  so this is private-set insurance, not a measured gain. It is worth having only because clearing a
  filter cannot lose the target; do not quote it as an improvement.
- **Intent override is now a closed question.** 29/30 with 25 at rank 1 and MTTC 0.09 turns off its
  floor, and the retraction routes are measured dead ends. Do not re-attempt C or D without new
  evidence — specifically, without evidence that the organizer changed `behavior_for` so that the
  override actually changes the target.
- **The remaining 4 non-rank-1 sessions belong to gap 1, not here.** They are ranks 2/2/7/4, and
  `public_0103` never moves off rank 4 at any point in the session. The lever is the coverage
  precision term in `starter/ranking.py` (`docs/features/09-optimization-headroom.md`), worth
  ~+0.0039 across just these four and ~+0.0348 across all 34 such sessions set-wide.
- **The disclosure schedule is right for these sessions and should be left alone.** Both sessions
  that withheld a top-10 target at the override turn gained rank by waiting.
